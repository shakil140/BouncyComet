What it is

A shelf of dice games for two

Dice Duo is a collection, not one game in five costumes. Each board has its own rule and its own kind of tension: a land grab, a tug of war, two races and a climb.

What they share is one die, two players, and rules short enough to explain while the other person is still sitting down.

The five games

Three steps each. That is the whole rulebook.

No tutorial to sit through and no glossary to memorise. Every game here is explained the way the app explains it: three short steps and one way to win.

Connect. Claim. Win.

Grid War

A cream board of dots, two colours of lines, and twenty-five boxes to fight over. The die decides how many lines you draw. Where you draw them is up to you.

  1. Roll for lines

    The die shows how many lines you draw this turn.

  2. Join the dots

    Tap between two dots to draw a line. Every line of your roll must be used.

  3. Close a box, claim it

    Draw the fourth side of a box and it is marked in your colour.

How you win: Most boxes wins. There are 25 of them, so a match never ends level.

Send them back

DiceBack

The game that started it all. A tray split corner to corner, your tokens on one side, theirs on the other, and every roll pushes a handful across the line.

  1. Set up

    Choose 3 to 20 tokens each. Both sides start equal.

  2. Roll and send

    Take turns. Send as many tokens as you roll over to the other side.

  3. Empty your side

    Rolled more than you have left? Send them all.

How you win: The first player with an empty side wins.

Roll. Mark. Race.

Roll Race

Two cards side by side, six rows each. No choices to agonise over: just the die, your card, and a rival filling theirs one mark faster or slower than you.

  1. Two marks per number

    Your card has rows 1 to 6, with two empty cells in each.

  2. Roll and mark

    Roll a number and an X goes into that row.

  3. Row full? Turn lost

    Nothing happens and play passes over.

How you win: The first player to fill all 12 cells wins.

Match your roll

Six Spots

One board cut into four triangles by a big X. Your triangle holds six numbered spots, and the die decides which one you get to cover next.

  1. Six spots, six tokens

    Your triangle holds the numbers 1 to 6.

  2. Roll and cover

    Roll the number of an empty spot and a token drops onto it.

  3. Already covered? Turn lost

    Nothing happens and play passes over.

How you win: The first player to cover all six spots wins.

Grow your total

Scoop Stack

Two cones, and a scoop for every roll. Each scoop carries your running total, so you watch the lead change hands all the way up the stack.

  1. Two cones, eight scoops

    Each of you fills a cone from the bottom up.

  2. Roll and add

    Your roll is added to your total, and the new total lands on your next scoop.

  3. Eight rolls each

    You both get exactly eight rolls, whoever fills their cone first.

How you win: The higher total wins. Level? A Cherry Roll decides it: one roll each, and the higher roll takes the cherry.

Ways to play

Four ways to start a match

Practise against the computer, hand the phone to the person next to you, get matched online, or open a private room. Same rules every time, completely different pressure.

In the app

Reasons to open it tomorrow

A match is quick. Everything around it is built so the next one is worth coming back for.

Dice and token skins

Dress your dice and your tokens. Starter styles come with the game, and more are bought with coins.

Daily reward

A reward waits every day you come back, and a streak grows for as long as you keep showing up.

Leaderboards and profile

Weekly and all-time boards for online play, and a profile that keeps your level and your progress.

Fair play

The same rules on both sides of the table and one shared die. Every roll is shown to both players, online it is made by the game server, and no match is ever played for real money.

Quick matches

Short enough for a bus ride, close enough to ask for a rematch. One tap and the next round starts.

Plays offline

No signal? Play the computer or pass the phone to a friend. Neither needs an account.

Questions

Straight answers about Dice Duo

The game is not public yet, so here is exactly where it stands today.Short answers to the things players ask us first.

Is Dice Duo free?

Yes. The download is free and no purchase is needed to play. Coins are collected by playing, from the daily reward and from optional rewarded ads, and they are spent inside the game: on match entry fees and on things like skins and boosters. Matches against the computer and online quick matches have a coin entry fee and pay coins to the winner. Two players on one phone is always free. Where purchases are offered, optional coin packs and one-time bundles are sold through Google Play, at the price Google Play shows. Coins and items have no cash value. Our Terms of Service and our Refunds and Cancellations page explain how purchases work.

Can I play with a friend on one phone?

Yes, in all five games. Choose two players, sit across from each other and take turns on the same screen. No second phone, no account and no connection.

Can I play a friend who is somewhere else?

Yes. Create a private room, send your friend its six-character code, and you are in the same match. You both need a free account and a connection.

Do I need an account?

Not to start. Playing the computer and playing two on one phone need no account. Online quick match, private rooms, the store, daily rewards and leaderboards need a free account, made with an email address or Google sign-in.

Is anything played for real money?

No. Dice Duo is a set of dice board games played for fun. Coins are game pieces with no cash value, whether you earn them or buy them: they cannot be withdrawn, sold or exchanged for money, and there are no cash prizes.

Who is it for?

Players aged 13 and over. The rules are simple enough to learn in one round, but the app has online play and ads, so it is not made for younger children.

Are the rolls fair?

Every roll is one fair six-sided die, and both players always see the result. In online matches the roll is made by the game server, never by either phone.

When does it launch, and where?

Dice Duo is on Google Play in closed testing, for Android: only invited testers can open its store page today. We have not announced a public release date, and we would rather open it when the matches feel right than name a date we have to walk back. Message us and we will tell you when it opens to everyone.

Where can I get it?

Dice Duo is on Google Play for Android. Get it on Google Play.
